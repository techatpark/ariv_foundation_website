The company is incorporated to collaborate with universities, academic institutions, and other educational bodies for the advancement of education, research, and technology skill-building.

That object is an invitation with a scope. Ariv is not looking for a logo on a poster. The collaboration has to advance education, research, or the building of technology skill — and it has to be readable against the first object, which is equal opportunity.

## Ways an institution can take part

- **Curricula.** Help develop, review, or teach with [TN eBooks](/work/tn-ebooks/) and [CSE notes](/work/cse-notes/), so free or low-cost material stays faithful to what students are actually examined and trained on.
- **Research and skill.** Joint work on technology skill-building, including laboratory practice that students can continue after the semester.
- **A consortium.** The training object allows a consortium of institutions and individuals, not only a centre the foundation runs alone. A department can teach with others.
- **Open source.** A course, a club, or a research group can contribute through the [Open Source Lab](/work/open-source-lab/), and meet a wider public at [DevCarnival](/work/devcarnival/).
- **A gathering.** Host or co-organise a seminar, workshop, or conference. Ticket sales, where they exist, stay reasonable and subject to law, and they support the charitable objects. See [Gatherings](/work/gatherings/).

## What to bring to the conversation

Write with something specific: who the learners are, what is already taught, and what should become shared. A syllabus, a laboratory course, a student community, or a research question is a better starting point than a general offer to partner.

How to send that note is on the [contact](/contact/) page.
